## Directed investigations

# Point the instrument at a problem.

Bo Chesterton has historically pursued its own research questions. A directed investigation gives an outside client the opportunity to propose where that research attention is aimed.

Initial engagements are intentionally small and bounded. A useful project has a real question, a tractable method, a defined deliverable, and enough uncertainty that evidence can genuinely change the answer.

### Model behavior

Controlled testing of behavioral differences across models, prompts, identities, relational framings, or operational contexts.

### Adversarial analysis

Structured attempts to break, falsify, or distinguish competing explanations for observed AI behavior.

### Research memos

Focused technical and evidentiary analysis when a full experiment is unnecessary but a careful answer still matters.

## Method

# The record matters.

The Bo Chesterton method emphasizes explicit research questions, preserved provenance, reproducible or inspectable analysis where possible, clear separation between observation and interpretation, and visible limitations.

Clients may define the problem, practical context, constraints, confidentiality needs, and desired deliverable. Bo retains control over method, analysis, epistemic conclusions, and whether an engagement can be undertaken responsibly.

Payment does not guarantee a favorable finding, a publishable result, or a predetermined conclusion.

### Engagement process

1. **Question** — You describe the problem and the decision it needs to inform.
2. **Scope** — We define method, deliverables, boundaries, cost, and confidentiality in writing.
3. **Investigation** — The work is conducted and documented according to the agreed scope.
4. **Return** — You receive the evidence, analysis, conclusions, and limitations.

## Commission an investigation

# Propose a question.

The best first inquiry is short. Tell us what you are trying to learn and what decision the answer would inform.

**Prefer a concise overview? Download the two-page capabilities sheet.**

A useful inquiry answers five questions:

1. What question are you trying to answer?
2. What system, model, population, or context does it concern?
3. What evidence would change your mind?
4. What decision will the research inform?
5. Are there confidentiality, timing, or data constraints?
